Fund Distribution in Switzerland: Third Party Marketing for Asset Managers

If you run long-only or UCITS strategies, one mandate connects you directly to 1 067 qualified prospects across the wholesale and institutional channels. You do not need a Swiss entity or a local hire, and you avoid the eighteen-month ramp-up.

Third party marketing vs. opening a Swiss office

Most foreign asset managers eventually have to choose between hiring a Swiss salesperson and outsourcing. The decision is usually framed as cost, but time and concentration risk matter just as much.

Marketing funds in Switzerland: who buys, and how

Swiss fund demand runs through two channels that share almost no common decision logic. In the wholesale channel, private banks select funds centrally for their buy-lists, while external asset managers and IFAs buy firm by firm and reward breadth of coverage. In the institutional channel, pension funds decide through investment consultants and committees, family offices allocate on conviction and referral, and insurers and cantonal banks follow balance-sheet requirements.

To market a fund in Switzerland you therefore work both channels at the same time: wholesale coverage produces earlier flows, while named institutional coverage takes a year or more to mature.

Wholesale channel: private banks, EAMs and IFAs

Roughly 70 private banks hold between CHF 2.8 and 3.1 trillion. Their fund selection is centralised and their distribution proprietary, so a single buy-list inclusion can move meaningful volume across thousands of client portfolios. Most foreign long-only managers underestimate this channel.

Alongside the banks sit 1 431 FINMA-licensed external asset managers and independent financial advisers, managing around CHF 887 billion. Each firm is small, but together they form one of the largest fund-buying populations in Europe. Here, results depend on breadth: contacting thirty EAMs produces little, while several hundred with consistent follow-up produce flows. In our reference mandate this segment generated the most activity, with 204 of the 470 investors reached.

Cantonal and retail banks reach a domestic client base that foreign managers rarely access directly.

Institutional channel

Over 1 000 pension funds hold roughly CHF 1.3 trillion. Most work through consultants, and their decision cycles run 12 to 18 months. 64 single family offices hold about CHF 1 trillion and allocate on conviction and referral. Insurers and cantonal banks add CHF 900 billion or more, driven by balance-sheet requirements and delegated mandates.

You cannot work these segments with wholesale tactics. You need named contacts, you need to know which consultants are involved, and you need to stay in the pipeline for a year or more — see accessing Swiss pension funds and Swiss family offices for how each behaves. We run both channels in parallel because wholesale produces earlier flows that fund the wait for institutional tickets, as the case study shows.

Business Development as a Service (BDaaS)

Third party marketing is often sold as introductions. We take on the whole business development function instead: it operates in your name and reports to you like an internal team. That is what we mean by BDaaS:

  • Market intelligence — who is allocating, to what, through which vehicle, and who advises them
  • Targeted outreach — positioned against what each allocator already holds
  • Meeting generation and attendance — senior, local, in the investor's language
  • DDQ and RFP support — where most foreign managers lose momentum after a good first meeting
  • CRM-tracked pipeline — every interaction logged and handed back to you at the end of the mandate
  • Feedback loop — what the market says about your pricing, share classes and positioning

The last point is frequently the most valuable in year one. Swiss allocators are direct about why they are not buying, and that information rarely reaches a head office in New York or London undiluted.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between third party marketing and a placement agent?

In practice the distinction is one of asset class and channel. Placement agents are associated with closed-ended private markets funds and the institutional channel. Third party marketing covers open-ended vehicles, usually UCITS and long-only strategies, and works both the wholesale and institutional channels on an ongoing basis rather than around a single fundraise. We do both — see placement agent Switzerland.

How much does third party marketing cost compared with a Swiss office?

A Swiss sales office means a senior salesperson, an entity, compliance and premises, and realistically 12 to 18 months before the first meaningful pipeline. A third party marketing mandate converts that fixed cost into a variable one, starts producing meetings within weeks, and covers all four Swiss regions instead of one.

Do you guarantee results?

We guarantee activity, not allocations. The volume of qualified outreach and meetings is written into the mandate and reported. No distributor can honestly guarantee an allocation, which depends on your track record, your vehicle and the allocator's own cycle.

What does a fund distributor in Switzerland do?

A fund distributor markets an asset manager’s funds to Swiss investors on the manager’s behalf: mapping which institutions can realistically invest, reaching the named decision-makers, generating and attending meetings, supporting DDQs and RFPs, and tracking the pipeline. Depending on the vehicle and the investor category, offering a fund may also require registration and appointed local agents — see UCITS distribution in Switzerland.

What is a marketing mandate?

A marketing mandate is the agreement under which a third party marketer represents a manager’s strategy in a market. Ours defines the target channels, writes the volume of qualified outreach into the contract and reports on it, and is one of three mandates at most, so your strategy is never presented alongside a competing one. When the mandate ends, the CRM-tracked pipeline is handed back to you.

Who distributes foreign funds in Switzerland?

Foreign managers have three routes: their own Swiss sales office or seconded salesperson, a placement agent for closed-ended private markets funds, or a third party marketer for open-ended UCITS and long-only strategies across the wholesale and institutional channels. Once a fund is selected, private banks then distribute it to their own clients through their buy-lists.
